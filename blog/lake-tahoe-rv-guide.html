Lake Tahoe RV Camping Guide: All Four Seasons

Everything Bay Area travelers need to RV camp at Lake Tahoe year-round — the best Class C campgrounds, cold-weather essentials, ski-resort access, and what changes from summer to snow.

Lake Tahoe is the rare Sierra destination that's spectacular in every season, and an RV is the best way to experience all of them. In summer you wake up steps from an alpine beach; in winter you park within minutes of world-class skiing with your own warm bed and kitchen waiting at the end of the day. This guide covers both — the best campgrounds, what changes by season, cold-weather essentials, and ski-resort access — written for Bay Area travelers heading up in a Class C.

Why Tahoe Is Worth the Drive in Any Season

From the Bay Area, Lake Tahoe is roughly 3.5 to 4 hours via I-80 (North Lake) or US-50 (South Lake). It's close enough for a long weekend and dramatic enough to feel like a real trip. In a Class C motorhome you skip the eye-watering Tahoe lodging rates — ski-season hotels near the resorts routinely run $300 to $600 a night — and you camp right where you want to be. All Crystal RV vehicles sleep five to six and are fully self-contained, which matters more here than almost anywhere else in California, because the weather swings hard.

Best Tahoe Campgrounds for a Class C

Tahoe Valley Campground (South Lake Tahoe)

Full hookups, open year-round, and close to Heavenly ski resort and the South Lake casinos. This is our top recommendation for winter trips specifically, because the electric hookups let you run your furnace and stay warm without draining the house battery on a sub-freezing night.

Fallen Leaf Campground (South Lake)

A gorgeous summer base near Fallen Leaf Lake and a short hop from Emerald Bay. Forested sites, open roughly late spring through fall. No hookups — ideal for a few self-contained summer nights.

Tahoe State Recreation Area (Tahoe City, North Lake)

Right in Tahoe City on the North Shore, walking distance to the lake and town. Compact sites that suit our 24–25-foot rigs well. Summer season.

Campground by the Lake (South Lake Tahoe)

City-run, central, and close to the beach and Heavenly. A practical, well-located summer option when the state parks are full.

Winter camping: the electric hookup rule

For any trip where overnight lows drop below freezing — routine at Tahoe from late fall through spring — book a campground with electric hookups. Running the RV furnace off propane is fine, but the furnace fan and your other systems draw on the house battery, which discharges fast in the cold. Shore power keeps the furnace running all night, keeps your batteries topped up, and means you wake up warm. Tahoe Valley Campground is the reliable year-round choice for this.

Winter RV Camping Essentials

Yes, you can absolutely RV camp in the snow at Tahoe — it's extraordinary. A few things make it comfortable and safe:

  • Book electric hookups (see above) so the furnace runs all night.
  • Carry tire chains and know how to fit them. Caltrans imposes chain controls on I-80 and US-50 during storms, and they're enforced. We can walk you through chain fitting at pickup.
  • Keep the fresh water and tanks managed. In hard freezes, run the furnace to keep the plumbing bay warm and don't leave the gray/black tanks sitting full.
  • Pack like you're skiing — because you are: layers, waterproof boots, gloves, and a warm sleeping setup (your RV bedding plus an extra blanket).
  • Watch the forecast. Sierra storms are serious. If a major system is rolling in, adjust your dates — a flexible plan beats a white-knuckle drive over Donner Pass.

Ski Resort Access

An RV base camp puts you minutes from Tahoe's biggest mountains. From the South Shore, Heavenly is right in South Lake Tahoe and Kirkwood is about 45 minutes south. From the North Shore, Palisades Tahoe and Northstar are the marquee resorts near Truckee and Tahoe City. Parking a motorhome at the resorts during peak days can be tight — the smarter play is to camp at a hookup site like Tahoe Valley and rideshare or shuttle to the lifts, then come home to a warm RV and a home-cooked dinner instead of a $90 slope-side meal.

Summer at the Lake

Come summer, Tahoe is a different and equally wonderful trip. The lake warms enough to swim by July, the campgrounds along the shore open up, and the hiking is superb — Emerald Bay, Eagle Lake, and the Rubicon Trail are all classics. Bring or rent paddleboards and kayaks; many shoreline campgrounds put you right on the water. This is the easy, family-friendly version of Tahoe, and our pet-friendly 2021 Thor Daybreak makes it a great trip to bring the dog.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you RV camp at Lake Tahoe in winter?

Yes. The key is booking a campground with electric hookups, such as Tahoe Valley Campground in South Lake Tahoe, which stays open year-round. Shore power lets your RV furnace run all night without draining the house battery in sub-freezing temperatures. You'll also need tire chains, which Caltrans requires during Sierra storms on I-80 and US-50.

How far is Lake Tahoe from the Bay Area by RV?

Lake Tahoe is roughly 3.5 to 4 hours from the Bay Area — via I-80 to the North Shore or US-50 to the South Shore. In winter, allow extra time for chain controls and slower mountain driving over Donner Pass.

What are the best RV campgrounds at Lake Tahoe?

Top picks include Tahoe Valley Campground (full hookups, year-round, near Heavenly), Fallen Leaf Campground (forested summer sites near Emerald Bay), Tahoe State Recreation Area in Tahoe City on the North Shore, and Campground by the Lake in South Lake Tahoe. All accommodate Crystal RV's 24-to-25-foot Class C motorhomes.

Can I ski at Tahoe and stay in an RV?

Absolutely. Camp at a hookup site like Tahoe Valley and you're minutes from Heavenly and Kirkwood on the South Shore, or Palisades Tahoe and Northstar near the North Shore. Rather than parking the motorhome at the resort, most guests shuttle or rideshare to the lifts and return to a warm RV and a home-cooked dinner.
