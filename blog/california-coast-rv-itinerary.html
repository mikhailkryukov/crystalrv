California Coast RV Road Trip: A 4-Day Highway 1 Itinerary

Highway 1 from San Francisco through Big Sur to Morro Bay — a relaxed four-day coastal itinerary with overnight stops, RV-friendly campgrounds, and where to actually park a Class C.

The Pacific Coast Highway is the road trip every Californian means to take and somehow never does. The good news: from the Bay Area it's a long weekend, not a two-week expedition. In four days you can drive Highway 1 from San Francisco down through Big Sur to Morro Bay, sleep with the ocean outside your window every night, and be home in time for Monday.

This is the exact 4-day coastal itinerary we hand to renters picking up a Class C from our Mountain View lot. It's paced for a relaxed trip — real driving distances, where to actually park an RV (not every Big Sur turnout fits a 25-foot motorhome), the campgrounds worth booking ahead, and the stops that earn the detour.

Why Highway 1 Is Perfect for a Class C

Coastal California was built for the RV traveler who doesn't want to commit to two weeks. The classic San Francisco-to-Morro-Bay stretch is roughly 230 miles of coastline, which sounds short until you remember Highway 1 through Big Sur averages 35 mph. That slowness is the point. In a Class C motorhome you carry your hotel, kitchen, and bathroom with you, so the lack of lodging along the wildest stretches stops being a problem and becomes the whole appeal.

Every Crystal RV vehicle is 24 to 25 feet — long enough to sleep five or six comfortably, short enough to handle the curves and fit the coastal campgrounds and state-park loops that turn away the big 35-foot rigs. That sizing is exactly why we steer first-time coastal road-trippers toward this route.

A note on Highway 1 closures

Good news for 2026: Caltrans reopened Highway 1 at Regent's Slide on January 14, 2026, so the full Carmel-to-Cambria drive is open for the first time in years. Big Sur is still landslide country, and storms close a section of road most winters somewhere between Carmel and San Simeon. Always check Caltrans QuickMap the day before you leave. If a section is closed, the fix is simple: drive the open portion, then cut inland via US-101 to rejoin the coast farther south. We'll flag any active closure when you pick up your RV.

Day 1: San Francisco to Santa Cruz

Drive: ~75 miles · 2 hours of relaxed driving

Pick up your fully stocked RV in Mountain View (or have us deliver it) and point south. Your first taste of the coast is at Half Moon Bay — stop for the bluff-top walk and an early lunch. From there, Highway 1 hugs the cliffs through Pescadero and past the Pigeon Point Lighthouse, one of the most photographed spots on the whole route.

Roll into Santa Cruz by mid-afternoon. Walk the Beach Boardwalk, watch the surfers at Steamer Lane, and grab dinner before settling in. Where to park: New Brighton State Beach in Capitola has bluff-top sites overlooking Monterey Bay and fits Class C rigs easily. Book ahead through ReserveCalifornia.

Day 2: Santa Cruz to Big Sur

Drive: ~70 miles · 2.5 hours with stops

This is the day the trip becomes the trip. Start with the 17-Mile Drive through Pebble Beach if you want the manicured version of the coast, then push on to the Monterey Peninsula. Carmel-by-the-Sea is worth an hour — park on the edge of town, the streets are tight for an RV.

South of Carmel, Highway 1 opens into Big Sur proper. The must-stops, all RV-friendly with roadside or lot parking: Bixby Creek Bridge (the iconic shot), Point Sur, and McWay Falls in Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park — an 80-foot waterfall dropping onto the beach, reached by a short walk from the parking area.

Where to park: Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park has wooded sites among the redwoods, a quintessential Big Sur night. It accommodates RVs up to about 27 feet and has no hookups, which is no issue for a few nights in any Crystal RV vehicle. Reserve early — Big Sur sites are some of the most competitive in the state.

Day 3: Big Sur to Morro Bay

Drive: ~95 miles · 3 hours with stops

The wildest, most remote stretch of the whole route. Fuel up before you leave Big Sur — gas stations are sparse and pricey through here. Highlights as you head south: the elephant seal rookery at Piedras Blancas (just north of San Simeon, free viewing boardwalk, hundreds of seals year-round) and Hearst Castle if you want to tour it (book tickets in advance).

By late afternoon you'll reach Morro Bay, anchored by the unmistakable Morro Rock. It's a laid-back fishing town with great seafood and an easy harbor walk — the perfect place to slow down. Where to park: Morro Bay State Park has a popular campground with some hookup sites right on the bay; Morro Strand State Beach is another solid option a few minutes north.

Day 4: Morro Bay Home

Drive: ~210 miles · ~3.5 hours via US-101

Slow morning, coffee made in your own kitchen, one last walk along the Embarcadero. For the trip home, skip the slow coastal road and take US-101 north — it's a fast, easy inland drive through Paso Robles wine country and Salinas, back to the Bay Area by early afternoon. If you have time, a stop in Paso Robles for a late lunch is a worthy send-off.

RV Driving Tips for Highway 1

  • Use the turnouts. California law requires slow vehicles to use turnouts when five or more cars stack up behind you. On Big Sur's curves, this keeps everyone happy — and it gives you the best photo pull-offs.
  • Drive it northbound-aware, park it southbound. Heading south puts you in the lane closest to the ocean, which makes pulling into viewpoints easy and safe.
  • Fuel up in towns. Carmel, then Morro Bay. The Big Sur stretch has little fuel and what exists is expensive.
  • Go slow at dawn and dusk. Deer and fog are both common on the coast. A Class C at 35 mph is exactly the right speed for this road anyway.
  • Download offline maps. Cell coverage drops to nothing through much of Big Sur.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drive Highway 1 from San Francisco to Morro Bay in an RV?

The San Francisco-to-Morro-Bay coastal stretch is roughly 230 miles, but Highway 1 through Big Sur averages about 35 mph, so plan on three to four days to do it comfortably with stops. The return trip home via inland US-101 is about 210 miles and takes around 3.5 hours.

Can a Class C motorhome handle the Big Sur section of Highway 1?

Yes. All Crystal RV motorhomes are 24 to 25 feet, which is ideal for Big Sur — long enough to be comfortable but short enough to handle the curves and fit coastal state-park campgrounds that turn away 35-foot rigs. Use turnouts when traffic builds behind you and drive the curves at a relaxed 35 mph.

Where can you camp along the California coast in an RV?

Top RV-friendly campgrounds on this route include New Brighton State Beach near Santa Cruz, Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park among the redwoods, and Morro Bay State Park with bayfront hookup sites. Reserve early through ReserveCalifornia, especially for Big Sur, which is among the most competitive campgrounds in California.

Is Highway 1 ever closed through Big Sur?

Highway 1 through Big Sur is fully open: Caltrans reopened it at Regent's Slide on January 14, 2026. Landslides can still close a section between Carmel and San Simeon after winter storms, so check Caltrans QuickMap before you leave. If a section is closed, drive the open portion and cut inland via US-101 to rejoin the coast farther south.
