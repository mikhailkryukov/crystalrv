RV Dump Station Guide: How to Empty Your Tanks the Right Way

The part of RVing nobody talks about — and it's genuinely simple once you've done it once. Here's the correct order, step by step, plus where to find dump stations and the mistakes to avoid.

Let's be honest: emptying the tanks is the task every new RVer dreads. Here's the reassurance — it's clean, simple, and takes about ten minutes once you know the steps. Do it in the right order and you'll never have an issue. This guide walks through exactly how, the way we explain it to first-time renters at pickup.

First, Know Your Three Tanks

  • Fresh water tank — clean water for your sink and shower. You fill this; it has nothing to do with dumping.
  • Gray water tank — used water from the sinks and shower. Soapy, not sewage.
  • Black water tank — waste from the toilet. This is the one people worry about, and it's still no big deal with a closed system.

You'll dump the black tank first, then the gray — the reason why is the whole trick, and we'll get to it.

What You Need (We Provide It)

Every Crystal RV comes with the gear, so you don't buy anything:

  • A sewer hose with fittings (on board)
  • Disposable gloves (bring a box — single-use is nicer here)
  • The fresh-water hose stays separate and is never used for dumping
  • Hand sanitizer or soap for after
One hard rule

Keep your fresh-water hose and your sewer hose completely separate, and never let them touch. The fresh hose (usually white or blue) fills your drinking water; the sewer hose handles waste. This is the one thing you never want to mix up.

Step-by-Step: Dumping Your Tanks

  1. Park next to the dump station with your waste outlet near the inlet. Put on gloves.
  2. Connect the sewer hose to the RV's waste outlet, twisting it to lock. Put the other end firmly into the dump station inlet — many have a threaded fitting or a weighted ring to hold it.
  3. Confirm both ends are secure before you open anything. This is the moment to double-check.
  4. Pull the BLACK tank valve first. Let it drain completely — you'll hear it slow to a trickle.
  5. Close the black valve, then pull the GRAY valve. The gray (soapy) water flushes the hose clean of anything the black tank left behind.
  6. Close the gray valve once it's drained.
  7. Rinse if a non-potable rinse water supply is available (see below), then disconnect the sewer hose, letting any last bit drain into the inlet.
  8. Stow the hose in its dedicated compartment, remove gloves, and wash or sanitize your hands.

Why Order Matters: Black Then Gray

This is the single most useful thing to remember. You always dump the black tank first, then the gray, because the gray water is relatively clean and soapy — running it through the hose last rinses out the black-tank residue. Do it in reverse and you'd leave the dirty hose dirty. Black, then gray. Every time.

Rinsing & Tank Care

  • Many dump stations have a non-potable water spigot for rinsing — use that, never the drinking-water tap, for hose rinsing
  • Keep your black tank valve closed while camping and only dump when it's about two-thirds full or more; this gives enough liquid to flush solids out cleanly
  • Use plenty of water with each flush and the RV-safe toilet treatment to keep things moving and odor-free
  • Only ever put down the toilet what's meant to go there, plus RV/marine-safe toilet paper

Where to Find Dump Stations

  • Campgrounds and RV parks — most have a dump station, and full-hookup sites let you dump right at your spot
  • Many gas stations and travel centers on RV routes, sometimes for a small fee
  • Some rest areas and state/national parks
  • Apps and sites that map dump stations make finding one on the road easy — search before you're full, not after

For California trips, you'll find dump stations at most of the campgrounds in our Bay Area campgrounds guide and along the routes in our coast itinerary.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Dumping gray before black — leaves the hose dirty; always black first
  • Leaving the black valve open while hooked up at a site — liquids drain but solids pile up; keep it closed and dump when full
  • Not securing the hose ends — the messy mistake; lock both ends before opening any valve
  • Using too little water — under-flushing causes clogs and odor; be generous
  • Mixing up fresh and sewer hoses — never; keep them stored apart

Frequently Asked Questions

What order do you dump RV tanks?

Always dump the black (toilet) tank first, then the gray (sink and shower) tank. The gray water is soapy and relatively clean, so draining it last rinses the hose of any black-tank residue. Black first, gray second — every time.

Is dumping an RV tank gross or hard?

It's neither, with a closed system and the right steps. You wear gloves, connect a sewer hose with both ends locked, pull the valves in order, and rinse. The whole job takes about ten minutes and you never come into contact with anything. First-timers are usually surprised how simple it is.

Where can I empty my RV tanks?

At campground and RV-park dump stations, many gas stations and travel centers on RV routes, and some rest areas and parks. Full-hookup campsites let you dump right at your site. Apps that map dump stations make it easy to find one before your tanks are full.

Should I leave my black tank valve open when hooked up?

No. Keep the black valve closed while camping, even with full hookups, and only dump when the tank is roughly two-thirds full or more. Leaving it open lets liquid drain while solids accumulate, which leads to clogs. Closed tank, dumped when full, is the rule.

Can I use regular toilet paper in an RV?

Use RV- or marine-safe toilet paper, which breaks down quickly and protects your black tank and plumbing from clogs. Regular household paper can be slow to dissolve. Crystal RV will confirm what's stocked and recommended for your rental.
