Winter RV Trips from the Bay Area

Eight trips we'd take between November and March: elephant seals, monarchs, a fully reopened Big Sur, snow at Tahoe, and desert sun in Death Valley.

Most people put the RV away when the rain starts. That's exactly why winter is one of our favorite seasons to rent: campgrounds empty out, the coast turns green, wildlife season peaks, and a motorhome with a furnace beats a cold tent every time. Here are the trips we'd take from the Bay Area between November and March, from a one-night seal walk to a week in Death Valley.

1. Elephant Seals at Año Nuevo

Año Nuevo State Park, on Highway 1 between Half Moon Bay and Santa Cruz, has one of the largest mainland northern elephant seal colonies in the world. During breeding season, from December 15 to March 31, you can only reach the colony on a docent-led guided walk. Book on ReserveCalifornia as early as you can, because weekend slots go fast.

  • The walk is 3–4 miles over sand dunes and takes about two and a half hours
  • No pets are allowed in the park, so plan dog care if four-legged co-pilots are along
  • Pair it with a night on the San Mateo or Santa Cruz coast. See our Bay Area campground picks

2. Monarch Butterflies in Santa Cruz

Natural Bridges State Beach on Santa Cruz's West Cliff Drive is California's only State Monarch Preserve. Overwintering monarchs cluster in its eucalyptus grove from roughly mid-October through late January, and they're most active on mild, sunny afternoons. An accessible boardwalk leads to the viewing deck. It's an easy one-nighter: monarchs in the morning, the Boardwalk and wharf in the afternoon.

3. Big Sur, Fully Reopened

For the first time in years, you can drive the whole Big Sur coast. Caltrans reopened Highway 1 at Regent's Slide on January 14, 2026, restoring the full route from Carmel to Cambria. Winter is when Big Sur is quietest and greenest. It's also when storms can still cause temporary closures, so check Caltrans QuickMap the morning you leave and travel between storm systems.

Our 4-day Highway 1 itinerary works in winter with shorter driving days, and our Big Sur RV rental page covers campgrounds that fit a Class C.

4. Pinnacles National Park

Summer at Pinnacles is hot. Winter and spring are the park's best seasons, with cool hiking weather on the High Peaks, talus caves to explore, and a good chance of seeing California condors. It's about 90 minutes from Mountain View, which makes it the Bay Area's easiest national park weekend. Everything you need is in our Pinnacles RV guide.

5. Snow Season at Lake Tahoe

A motorhome makes a great ski-trip basecamp, as long as you respect Sierra winter driving. Caltrans posts chain controls on I-80 and US-50 during storms:

  • R1: chains, traction devices or snow tires required on the drive axle (4WD/AWD excepted)
  • R2: chains or traction devices required on all vehicles except 4WD/AWD with snow tires on all four wheels
  • R3: chains required on all vehicles, no exceptions (roads often close before this)

Our Class C motorhomes aren't four-wheel drive, so plan to chain up whenever chain controls are posted, and always follow the signs and Caltrans or CHP staff at the checkpoint. Every vehicle entering a chain-control area must carry chains. Crystal RV includes chains on request at no charge, and we'll show you how to fit them at pickup. Choose a campground with electric hookups so you can run the heat without worrying about the batteries, and read our four-season Tahoe guide before you book.

6. Wine Country in the Off-Season

After harvest, Napa and Sonoma slow down. Tasting rooms are calmer, reservations are easier, and the vineyards turn gold and then green with the first rains. A motorhome means nobody has to worry about getting back to a hotel. Park at a campground, walk or rideshare to dinner, and wake up in the vines. See our Napa Valley RV rental page.

7. Whale Season on the Mendocino Coast

Gray whales migrate along the North Coast in winter and early spring, and the bluffs around Point Cabrillo and the Mendocino Headlands are classic lookouts. Take the CA-128 route through Anderson Valley and base yourself at MacKerricher State Park near Fort Bragg. Its sites take RVs up to 35 feet. In the off-season, some loops close, so check what's open before you go. Route and campground details are on our Mendocino RV rental page.

8. The Big One: Death Valley

For a week-long winter escape, Death Valley is the classic RV trip. From mid-April to mid-October it's too hot for most campers, but November through March brings pleasant days, cold clear nights, and some of the darkest skies in the country. It's a long drive (plan on about eight hours from the Bay Area), and Tioga Pass through Yosemite is closed in winter, so take the southern route.

  • Furnace Creek Campground takes reservations for October 15 – April 15 on Recreation.gov, up to six months ahead
  • Only 18 sites have full hookups, and they're often booked months in advance
  • Plan at least five nights so the drive doesn't eat the trip

Winter RV Tips from Our Fleet

Staying warm, dry, and unfrozen

Run the propane furnace overnight and keep a window cracked for ventilation. On nights forecast below freezing, disconnect and drain the outside water hose so it can't freeze solid, and keep the furnace on so the tanks and lines stay warm. Some of our RVs have 12V tank heaters for extra protection. Expect more condensation inside in winter. A quick wipe of the windows each morning keeps it in check.

  • Days are short. Plan to arrive at camp before dark, especially on a new campground
  • Many coastal and mountain campgrounds close loops in the off-season, so confirm what's open when you book
  • Propane runs the furnace, stove and fridge, so watch the gauge on longer cold trips
  • Pack layers, rain gear and a doormat-sized towel for muddy shoes
  • Check Caltrans QuickMap for closures and chain controls before every driving day

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you rent an RV in winter in the Bay Area?

Yes. Crystal RV rents year-round from Mountain View, and winter is our quietest season, so weekends are much easier to book. Our motorhomes have propane furnaces for cold nights, and chains are available on request at no charge for mountain trips.

When can you see the elephant seals at Año Nuevo?

The breeding-season guided walks run from December 15 to March 31. During that period, the colony is only reachable on a docent-led walk, which you reserve through ReserveCalifornia. Pets aren't allowed in the park.

Is Highway 1 through Big Sur open?

Yes. Caltrans reopened Highway 1 at Regent's Slide on January 14, 2026, restoring the full route between Carmel and Cambria. Winter storms can still cause temporary closures, so check Caltrans QuickMap before you set out.

Do I need chains to drive an RV to Lake Tahoe in winter?

During storms, Caltrans imposes chain controls on I-80 and US-50, and every vehicle entering a chain-control area must carry chains. Crystal RV includes chains on request at no charge and covers fitting at pickup.

How do I keep an RV from freezing on a winter trip?

Keep the furnace running on cold nights, disconnect and drain the outside water hose when temperatures drop below freezing, and stay at a site with electric hookups when you can. Some of our RVs also have 12V tank heaters.
