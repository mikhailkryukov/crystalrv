If you're thinking about renting an RV from the Bay Area for a Yosemite trip, you're making one of the best calls in California travel. The drive is short, the parking is your accommodation, and the views from your dinette window beat any hotel in the state.

This guide is everything we tell first-time renters before they leave our Mountain View lot for Yosemite. It covers the 2026 park rules, exactly which campgrounds fit Class C motorhomes, when to book (this is the #1 thing people get wrong), and a 4-day itinerary that works for families, couples, and groups.

Why Yosemite Is Made for an RV Trip

Yosemite has roughly 4 million annual visitors and almost none of them have their own bed, kitchen, and bathroom on-site. That's the RV advantage. Hotel rooms inside the park are scarce and expensive — typically $400 to $700 per night for anything decent at The Ahwahnee, Yosemite Valley Lodge, or Curry Village. Hotels outside the park add a 45 to 90 minute drive each way, which means you're either rushing to leave before dawn or missing sunset.

With a Class C motorhome, you camp inside the park at Upper Pines, Lower Pines, or North Pines. You wake up to granite walls, walk to Yosemite Falls in 20 minutes, and you have a full kitchen so you're not paying $24 for a mediocre burger at the lodge cafeteria three times a day.

The other underrated benefit: kids and dogs. Every Crystal RV vehicle sleeps 5 to 6 and the 2021 Thor Daybreak is pet-friendly. If you've ever tried to find a pet-friendly hotel in Yosemite Valley with a king bed and a sleeper sofa, you know what we mean.

2026 Park Entry Rules (No Timed Reservations)

Good news for 2026: Yosemite does not require a timed-entry reservation this year. From 2020 through 2025, the National Park Service used various reservation systems to control crowds, and many Bay Area travelers still think they need one. They don't.

Here's what you actually need:

  • Park entry fee: $35 per vehicle, valid for 7 consecutive days. Cash, card, or America the Beautiful annual pass ($80, worth it if you visit 2+ national parks per year).
  • Campground reservation: Required for all overnight stays inside the park, year-round. Book through Recreation.gov.
  • Tioga Pass: Closes seasonally (typically mid-November to late May). Check current status if planning to enter from the east via Highway 120 east.

Park hours are 24/7, and the entrance gates are staffed during peak hours. If you arrive late at night, your campground reservation entitles you to enter — just have the confirmation email accessible on your phone.

Best Yosemite Campgrounds for Class C RVs

Yosemite has 13 campgrounds, but only a handful are practical for Class C motorhomes in the 24 to 27 foot range (which is the entire Crystal RV fleet). Here's the honest breakdown:

Upper Pines (Best for First-Time RVers)

RV limit: 35 feet · Open: Year-round · Sites: 238

The biggest and most accessible campground in Yosemite Valley. Paved roads, easy back-in sites, flush toilets, and within walking distance of the shuttle bus, Half Dome trailhead, Mirror Lake, and Curry Village. This is where we send 80% of first-time RV renters. The downside: it books up fastest. Sites have a fire ring, picnic table, and bear-proof food storage locker. No hookups — you'll run on battery and water tanks, which is fine for 3 to 4 nights in any Crystal RV vehicle.

Lower Pines

RV limit: 40 feet · Open: March through October · Sites: 60

Smaller and more relaxed than Upper Pines, with some sites right along the Merced River. Same amenities, same walking access to Curry Village and Yosemite Village. Books out almost as fast.

North Pines

RV limit: 40 feet · Open: April through October · Sites: 81

The quietest of the three Pines campgrounds, set further back from the village hub. Best for couples or families who want a more peaceful base. Walking distance to Mirror Lake.

Wawona

RV limit: 35 feet · Open: Year-round · Sites: 93

Located near the south entrance, about 45 minutes from Yosemite Valley. Better availability than the Valley campgrounds and great if you want to focus on Mariposa Grove (the giant sequoias) and Glacier Point.

Hodgdon Meadow

RV limit: 35 feet · Open: Year-round · Sites: 105

Near the Big Oak Flat entrance (Highway 120 west — the route from the Bay Area). Less scenic than Valley campgrounds but a solid base if you want easier access in and out and don't mind the 30-minute drive to the Valley each morning.

Why "no hookups" doesn't matter

First-time renters often worry about boondocking (camping without water, power, or sewer hookups) in Yosemite. Don't. Every Crystal RV vehicle has a 40+ gallon fresh water tank, a deep-cycle battery, a propane stove and fridge, and gray/black tanks that hold a week of light use. For a 3 to 4 night Yosemite trip, you'll have power for lights, water for showers (be efficient), and tanks that won't fill up. We'll cover all this in your orientation.

When and How to Book Campsites

This is the section that, more than any other, determines whether your Yosemite RV trip happens.

Yosemite Valley campsites release exactly 5 months in advance on the 15th of each month at 7:00 AM Pacific Time through Recreation.gov. For a July 15-18 trip, reservations open February 15 at 7:00 AM PT.

Summer weekend sites in Upper Pines, Lower Pines, and North Pines sell out in under 60 seconds. We're not exaggerating. Here's how to actually get one:

  1. Create a Recreation.gov account beforehand. Verify your email, save your payment info, and add the campground to your favorites.
  2. Set a calendar reminder for 6:55 AM PT on the release date.
  3. Open the campground page at exactly 6:58 AM and have the dates pre-filled.
  4. Refresh at 7:00 AM and click the first available site. Don't deliberate. Any site is better than no site.
  5. Complete payment within 15 minutes or your hold expires.

If you miss the initial release, all hope is not lost. Cancellations happen constantly. Check Recreation.gov daily, especially 3 to 4 weeks before your dates. We've also had renters score sites the night before by checking at midnight when the next-day's cancellation window closes.

Shoulder season hack: April, October, and early November have far better availability and Yosemite is arguably more beautiful with fewer crowds. Waterfalls peak in April and May. Fall foliage hits in mid-October. We strongly recommend these months for first-timers.

Driving from the Bay Area to Yosemite

From Mountain View (where you'll pick up your Crystal RV — or where we'll deliver it to you), Yosemite Valley is about 170 miles, or 4 hours of driving in a Class C motorhome at relaxed speeds.

The two main routes:

  • Highway 120 via Manteca and Big Oak Flat (recommended): Faster and less curvy. Take I-580 east to I-205, then Highway 120 east through Manteca, Oakdale, and Groveland to the Big Oak Flat entrance. This is the easiest drive for first-time RVers.
  • Highway 140 via Mariposa: Slightly longer but considered more scenic, following the Merced River for the last hour. Take Highway 99 south to Highway 140 east. Good if you want a slower, prettier drive.

A few RV-specific tips:

  • Fill up in Oakdale or Mariposa. Gas inside the park costs roughly 50% more than Bay Area prices.
  • Use the right lane on highway grades. A Class C with full tanks doing 55 on an uphill is fine — let cars pass.
  • Watch for low-clearance bridges. All Crystal RV vehicles are well under 11 feet tall, so this is rarely an issue, but be aware in older neighborhoods near park towns.
  • Once inside the park, drive slowly. Wildlife is everywhere — bears, deer, coyotes — especially at dawn and dusk. Speed limits are 35 mph or lower on Valley roads for a reason.

Suggested 4-Day Yosemite RV Itinerary

This itinerary assumes you're staying in Upper Pines and have a Class C motorhome. It works for families with kids, couples, and groups of 4 to 6.

Day 1: Arrival and Yosemite Valley Floor

Leave the Bay Area by 9:00 AM. Stop in Oakdale or Groveland for lunch, ideally somewhere with easy RV parking. Arrive at Upper Pines by 3:00 PM, set up your site (electric hookup setup is optional — we'll show you), then walk to Lower Yosemite Fall for a sunset hike. Easy 1-mile loop, paved, kid-friendly. Cook dinner at your campsite.

Day 2: Iconic Day Hikes

Take the free Valley Shuttle to Mirror Lake (easy 2-mile loop) in the morning, then drive to Glacier Point (1 hour each way) for the iconic Half Dome and Valley overlook. Pack lunch from your RV kitchen. Back at camp by 5:00 PM. Sunset at Tunnel View — the famous panorama, 15 minutes from the Valley by RV.

Day 3: Big Trees and Waterfalls

Drive to Mariposa Grove (south end of the park, near Wawona, about 90 minutes from Upper Pines) to see the giant sequoias. Take the shuttle from the welcome plaza to the trees. Back to the Valley for an afternoon hike to Vernal Fall footbridge (1.6 miles round-trip, easy) or, for the ambitious, the full Mist Trail to the top of Vernal Fall (3 miles round-trip, strenuous).

Day 4: Departure with One Last View

Slow morning. Make coffee in your RV, walk around Curry Village, grab a final view at Tunnel View on your way out. Aim to leave the park by 11:00 AM to be back in the Bay Area for a relaxed dinner. If you have flexibility, stop in Groveland for lunch — it's a charming gold rush town with surprisingly good restaurants.

What to Pack (and What's Already in Your RV)

Every Crystal RV vehicle comes fully stocked — a critical detail that separates us from peer-to-peer rentals on Outdoorsy or RVshare. You don't need to bring kitchenware, bedding, towels, cleaning supplies, or basic outdoor gear.

Already in your RV:

  • Complete kitchen: pots, pans, plates, glasses, utensils, mugs, coffee maker, knife set, cutting board, dish soap, sponges, paper towels
  • All bedding: sheets, blankets, pillows for every bed
  • Bathroom: bath towels, hand towels, toilet paper, basic toiletries
  • Camping gear: 4 camping chairs, outdoor patio mat, broom and dustpan
  • Safety: fire extinguisher, smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, first aid kit, flashlight
  • Power: USB outlets, AC outlets when on shore power or generator, deep-cycle house battery

What you should bring for Yosemite specifically:

  • Layers: even summer Valley evenings drop to the 50s. Pack a warm jacket and long pants for nights.
  • Hiking shoes or sturdy sneakers (don't try Mist Trail in flip-flops)
  • Reusable water bottles — drinking water spigots are throughout the campgrounds
  • Cash for the park entrance ($35) if you don't use the America the Beautiful pass
  • Pre-downloaded offline maps — cell signal is spotty in the Valley and nonexistent on the Tioga Road
  • Bear-aware snacks: anything with strong smells stays in the bear box at your campsite, never in the RV overnight
  • Food for the trip — there's a small store in Yosemite Valley but selection is limited and prices are 2x to 3x Bay Area grocery prices. Stock up before you go.

Common Mistakes First-Time Yosemite RVers Make

Honest mistakes we see repeatedly:

  1. Booking too late. If your trip is in peak season (June-August) and reservations have been open for more than a week, you've missed the easy window. Shift to shoulder season or aim for Wawona/Hodgdon Meadow.
  2. Underestimating drive time. Class C RVs go 55-60 mph on highways, not 75. Your Google Maps ETA is for cars and is optimistic. Add 30%.
  3. Forgetting bear protocols. All food, toiletries, and scented items go in the bear box at your site, never inside the RV at night. Yosemite bears know how to peel back RV roofs like sardine cans. We will absolutely cover this in your orientation but it's worth saying twice.
  4. Trying to see everything. Yosemite is bigger than Rhode Island. Pick 2-3 areas and enjoy them slowly rather than racing between Glacier Point, Mariposa Grove, and Tuolumne Meadows in 48 hours.
  5. Skipping the orientation. Our RV orientation takes 45-60 minutes for first-time renters. Don't try to shortcut it. Knowing how to dump tanks, operate the generator, and use the awning will save you from texting us frantically from a campsite at 11 PM.
  6. Bringing too much. Class C motorhomes have ample storage but it's not unlimited. One soft duffel per person plus a shared cooler is plenty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a timed-entry reservation to enter Yosemite in 2026?

No. Yosemite National Park does not require a timed-entry reservation in 2026. You only need a campground reservation if you plan to stay overnight in the park, and a standard $35 park entry fee per vehicle (valid 7 days).

What size RV can fit in Yosemite campgrounds?

Most Yosemite campgrounds accept RVs up to 35 feet, but the easiest-driving spots favor RVs under 27 feet. All Crystal RV Class C motorhomes are 24-25 feet, which fits comfortably in Upper Pines, Lower Pines, North Pines, and Wawona campgrounds without issue.

How far in advance should I book a Yosemite RV campsite?

Reservations open exactly 5 months in advance on the 15th of each month at 7am PT through Recreation.gov for the prime May-September season. Summer weekends sell out in under 60 seconds. Book the moment reservations open or aim for shoulder season (April, October, early November) when same-week availability is common.

How long does it take to drive from the Bay Area to Yosemite in an RV?

From the San Francisco Bay Area to Yosemite Valley is approximately 4 hours of driving in a Class C motorhome (170-180 miles via Highway 120 from Mountain View). Plan an extra 30-60 minutes for restroom and fuel stops with a family. The drive is straightforward — no mountain switchbacks until inside the park.
