The Complete RV Trip Checklist: Pack Smart, Forget Nothing

What to bring, what's already in the RV, and the pre-departure, setup, and breakdown steps that keep a trip running smoothly — built for first-time renters.

The biggest first-timer mistake isn't forgetting something — it's over-packing because you don't know what the RV already has. The good news: a stocked rental like ours covers most of the gear. This checklist sorts the "already handled" from the "you bring it," plus the routines that make setup painless.

What's Already in Your Crystal RV

Every Crystal RV goes out with 100+ items, so you can cross all of this off before you start packing:

  • Kitchen: pots, pans, plates, bowls, mugs, glasses, utensils, cooking tools, cutting board, can opener, coffee maker
  • Sleeping: bedding, sheets, pillows, and blankets for every berth
  • Bath: towels and basic supplies; the RV has its own toilet and shower
  • Camp gear: camp chairs, leveling blocks, wheel chocks, fresh-water hose, sewer hose, and a power adapter
  • Safety: fire extinguisher, smoke and CO detectors, first-aid basics, and roadside equipment
Rule of thumb

If it's hardware, gear, or kitchen equipment, assume it's already on board and ask us rather than buying it. If it's food, clothing, or a personal item, it's on you. That single distinction saves most renters a carload of unnecessary stuff.

What You Actually Need to Bring

  • Driver's license for each driver listed on the rental
  • Phone chargers and a 12V car-style adapter or USB block
  • Reusable water bottles
  • Layers — weather swings hard between the coast, valley, and mountains
  • Any prescription medication and a small personal first-aid kit
  • Campground and park reservation confirmations (screenshot them — signal gets thin)
  • A paper map or offline maps downloaded in advance
  • Trash bags, dish soap, and paper towels (we provide starters; bring extra for longer trips)

Food & Kitchen

The RV has the equipment; you bring the groceries. Plan meals before you shop so you're not hauling a week of food you won't eat in a fridge that's smaller than the one at home.

  • Plan breakfasts and dinners at camp, lunches on the road
  • Pre-chop and pre-marinate at home in sealed containers to save space and cleanup
  • Pack a cooler bag for drinks so the fridge stays free for perishables
  • Bring coffee/tea, cooking oil, salt, pepper, and your go-to spices in small containers
  • Foil, zip bags, and a couple of food-storage containers for leftovers

Clothing & Personal

  • Layers: a warm mid-layer and rain shell even in summer, especially near the coast or in the Sierra
  • Closed-toe shoes for hookups and campground walking, plus sandals
  • Hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, insect repellent
  • Quick-dry towel for beaches or lakes
  • Headlamp or flashlight (hands-free beats your phone for night hookups)

Pre-Departure Checks

We walk you through all of this at orientation, but here's the routine to repeat before every drive day:

  • Roof vents and windows closed and latched
  • Fridge switched to the right power mode and the door latched
  • Loose items stowed — nothing on counters that can fly during braking
  • TV, cabinets, drawers, and the bathroom door secured
  • Step retracted, exterior compartments latched, and power/water/sewer fully disconnected and stowed
  • Tire pressure looks right and mirrors are adjusted before you pull out

Arriving & Setting Up Camp

  1. Pull into your site and check you're roughly level before unbuckling — use leveling blocks under the low-side tires if needed
  2. Set the parking brake and chock the wheels
  3. Connect shore power first, then fresh water, then sewer (if your site has full hookups)
  4. Extend any slide-out only after you're parked and level
  5. Pop the awning, set out the camp chairs, and you're done

Breaking Camp

Reverse the setup, and do a final "walk-around" — the single habit that prevents leaving something behind or driving off still connected:

  1. Retract the awning and slide-outs; stow everything loose
  2. Disconnect sewer, then water, then power (reverse of hookup order)
  3. Walk a full lap around the RV: hoses stowed, compartments latched, step in, nothing left at the site
  4. Confirm the parking brake is the last thing released before you roll

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I need to pack for an RV trip if the RV is stocked?

With a fully stocked rental, you mainly bring food, clothing, toiletries, medications, phone chargers, and reservation confirmations. Kitchen equipment, bedding, towels, camp chairs, and hookup gear are already on board, so skip those.

Do I need to bring my own bedding and towels?

Not with Crystal RV — bedding, sheets, pillows, blankets, and towels are included for every berth. Many renters bring a favorite pillow, but it isn't required.

What's the most commonly forgotten item on an RV trip?

A 12V or USB charging adapter, offline maps, and layers for temperature swings are the three most-forgotten. Cell signal and weather both change fast in California, and people pack for the city they left, not the mountains they're driving to.

How much food should I bring?

Plan your meals first, then shop. RV fridges are smaller than home fridges, so buy for a few days at a time and restock en route. Pre-chopping and pre-marinating at home saves both space and cleanup.

Do you go over all of this at pickup?

Yes. Every Crystal RV rental includes a hands-on orientation covering hookups, the pre-departure checklist, setup, and breakdown, so you leave confident even if it's your first trip.
