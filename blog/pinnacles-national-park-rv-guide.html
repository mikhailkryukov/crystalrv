Pinnacles National Park RV Guide: The Bay Area's Closest National Park

Condors overhead, talus caves to scramble through, and spring wildflowers — all under two hours from Silicon Valley. Here's how to do Pinnacles in a Class C motorhome, including the campground details that trip people up.

Ask most Bay Area locals to name the nearest national park and they'll say Yosemite — a four-hour haul. The real answer is Pinnacles, less than two hours south of Silicon Valley, and almost nobody thinks of it. That's exactly what makes it the ideal first RV trip: close enough that a short drive gets you there with daylight to spare, small enough to feel manageable, and wild enough to feel like a real escape. Towering rock spires, caves you walk through, and California condors riding the thermals overhead.

Why Pinnacles Is the Perfect First RV Trip

For a first-timer behind the wheel of a motorhome, Pinnacles takes the pressure off. The drive from our Mountain View base is short and mostly straightforward, so you're not spending your first day white-knuckling a long mountain grade. The in-park campground has real RV sites with hookups. And because the park is compact, you can see the highlights in a weekend without a punishing schedule. It's the trip we'd point a nervous first renter toward before anything bigger.

Getting There: Use the East Entrance

From the Bay Area it's roughly 80 miles — about 1.5 to 2 hours — south past Gilroy and Hollister. Here's the one thing that catches people out: Pinnacles has two entrances that are not connected by any road through the park. The campground and the RV sites are all on the east side, reached via Highway 25. The west entrance near Soledad is day-use only, with no campground. So if you're bringing the RV to camp, you want the East Entrance off Highway 25 — don't let your GPS send you to the west side.

Quick navigation tip

Set your destination to the Pinnacles Campground / East Entrance on Highway 25, not just "Pinnacles National Park," which can route you to the disconnected west side. The two halves are joined only by hiking trails, not a drivable road.

Pinnacles Campground: What RVers Need to Know

There's exactly one campground in the park, and it's well set up for motorhomes:

  • RV sites with hookups: roughly three dozen RV sites offer 30-amp electric hookups — plenty for a Class C running lights, the fridge, and charging
  • On-site dump station and drinking water, so you can empty and fill before heading home
  • Flush toilets and showers, plus a camp store for ice, firewood, and basics
  • A swimming pool, open roughly April through September — a genuine luxury after a hot hike
  • Sites have picnic tables, fire rings, and food-storage lockers; our 24–25 foot motorhomes fit comfortably within the campground's RV length limits

Reserve early. Pinnacles Campground is reservation-based through Recreation.gov, bookable up to six months out, and spring and fall weekends fill fast. Book as soon as your dates are set, and check the official site for current rates and availability.

Important: No Generators Allowed

This is the detail that surprises RVers, so plan around it: generators are not permitted anywhere in Pinnacles Campground, at any time. That's not a problem if you book one of the electric-hookup RV sites — you plug in and you're set. If you end up at a non-hookup site, you'll be running off the house batteries only, which comfortably handle lights, the water pump, the fridge on gas, and phone charging for a night or two, but won't run the air conditioning. Given how hot Pinnacles gets, that hookup site is worth prioritizing. (For how RV power works generally, see our RV generator guide.)

What to Do: Caves, Condors & Peaks

  • Talus caves: Pinnacles' signature experience — caves formed by boulders wedged into narrow canyons, which you scramble and duck through with a flashlight. Bear Gulch and Balconies are the two cave areas. They can close seasonally to protect roosting bats or after rain, so check current conditions at the visitor center.
  • California condors: Pinnacles is one of the best places in the world to see these enormous endangered birds, with wingspans approaching ten feet. You can often spot them riding thermals right from the campground or the High Peaks.
  • The High Peaks: the classic hike, winding up among the rock spires with steep, railing-assisted sections near the top. Dramatic and very doable as a half-day loop.
  • Rock climbing: Pinnacles is a well-known climbing destination thanks to its volcanic spires, if that's your thing.
  • Wildflowers: in spring the trails and meadows fill with color — the park's prettiest season.

Bring more water than you think you need, real shoes, and a flashlight or headlamp for the caves (a perfect use for the one on your trip checklist).

When to Go (and When to Avoid)

  • Best: spring (March–May). Wildflowers, green hills, comfortable temperatures, active condors. It's also the busiest, so reserve well ahead.
  • Great: fall (Sept–Nov). Cooler, quieter, and the pool may still be open early in the season.
  • Tough: summer. Daytime temperatures regularly top 100°F. The park is far less crowded then, and the campground pool helps, but plan hikes for early morning and lean on your hookup site to keep the RV cool in the evening.
  • Winter: open year-round and pleasant on mild days, though caves may close after heavy rain.

A Simple 2-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Pick up the RV in the morning, make the easy drive down to the East Entrance, and set up at your hookup site by early afternoon. Walk the Bear Gulch area and through the cave (conditions permitting) to the reservoir, then watch for condors at golden hour from camp. Dinner at your picnic table, a dip in the pool if it's open.

Day 2: Start early before the heat and hike the High Peaks loop among the spires. Back to camp for lunch and the pool, then break camp, top off water and use the dump station, and make the short drive home — back in Silicon Valley by evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the closest national park to the Bay Area?

Pinnacles National Park is the closest, roughly 80 miles and about 1.5 to 2 hours south of Silicon Valley — much nearer than Yosemite, which is around four hours away. That proximity makes Pinnacles an ideal short RV trip and a great first outing for new motorhome drivers.

Can you camp in an RV at Pinnacles National Park?

Yes. Pinnacles Campground, on the east side of the park, has RV sites — about three dozen of which include 30-amp electric hookups — plus an on-site dump station, drinking water, showers, a camp store, and a seasonal swimming pool. Reserve through Recreation.gov, up to six months ahead, as spring and fall weekends book up fast.

Which Pinnacles entrance do I use with an RV?

Use the East Entrance off Highway 25. The campground and all RV sites are on the east side, and the east and west entrances are not connected by any road through the park — only by hiking trails. Set your GPS to the East Entrance or Pinnacles Campground specifically so it doesn't route you to the day-use west side near Soledad.

Are generators allowed at Pinnacles Campground?

No. Generators are not permitted anywhere in Pinnacles Campground at any time. Book one of the electric-hookup RV sites so you can plug in for power, including to run air conditioning in the heat. Non-hookup sites rely on house batteries only, which won't run the AC.

When is the best time to visit Pinnacles in an RV?

Spring (March through May) is the best overall — wildflowers, mild temperatures, and active condors, though it's also the busiest. Fall is cooler and quieter. Summer regularly exceeds 100°F, so visit then only with a hookup site for air conditioning and plan hikes for early morning; the campground pool helps in the heat.
