10 Best RV Campgrounds Near San Francisco

From oceanfront cliffs to redwood groves — the ten best places to take a Class C RV when you don't have time to make it to Yosemite or Tahoe.

Published April 22, 2026 · 8 min read · By Michael, Crystal RV founder
Home  ›  Trip Guides  ›  10 Best RV Campgrounds Within 3 Hours of San Francisco (2026)

You don't need to drive 5 hours to Yosemite or Tahoe for a real RV trip. Some of the best campgrounds in California are within a 3-hour radius of San Francisco — and most of them have better availability, easier driving, and equally stunning settings.

This is our honest list of the ten places we recommend most often to Crystal RV renters who want a short trip, a long weekend, or a "we have to be back in town Monday morning" adventure. Every one of these accommodates Class C motorhomes in the 24-25 foot range (our entire fleet).

1. Half Moon Bay State Beach Campground

Drive time from SF: 35 minutes · Hookups: No (dry camping) · Cost: ~$45/night

The closest "real" oceanfront RV camping to San Francisco. Sites are just yards from the Pacific, with the sound of waves all night. Francis Beach, Venice Beach, and Dunes Beach are all reachable on foot from the campsites. No hookups means you'll run on tanks for a few nights, which is totally fine in any Crystal RV vehicle.

Reservations are required (Reserve California) and weekend sites typically need to be booked 4-6 months ahead. Midweek availability is much better. The campground is open year-round and is honestly more pleasant in fall and winter when the Bay Area cools off and the crowds thin.

Best for: First-time RVers, families with kids, anyone who wants an ocean view without a long drive.

2. Pillar Point RV Park

Drive time from SF: 35 minutes · Hookups: Full (water, electric, sewer) · Cost: $90-120/night

A few miles south of Half Moon Bay, Pillar Point is the polished, full-amenity counterpart to the state beach. Full hookups at every site, clean restrooms with showers, laundry, Wi-Fi, and ocean views from the front row sites. The famous Mavericks surf break is right at the harbor. Walking access to the Half Moon Bay coast trail, which runs over 10 miles along the cliffs.

This is where we recommend renters go for their very first RV trip. The proximity to home, the full hookups, and the laid-back atmosphere make it a stress-free way to learn the ropes before tackling more remote sites.

Best for: First-time renters, families wanting comforts, weekend trips where you want to feel like you're on vacation without driving far.

3. Samuel P. Taylor State Park

Drive time from SF: 50 minutes · Hookups: No (some sites have water hookups) · Cost: $35/night

A redwood-shaded campground in West Marin, just past Lagunitas. Sites are spread out among second-growth redwoods along Lagunitas Creek. Quiet, beautiful, and easy to forget you're 50 miles from a major city. The campground is on the Cross Marin Trail, which is paved and great for biking — bring or rent e-bikes for an easy ride to the coast.

The downside: redwood roots and tight turns mean some sites are tricky for RVs over 26 feet. Crystal RV's fleet (24-25 feet) fits fine in most sites, but pay attention to site length when booking.

Best for: Couples and small families, anyone who loves redwoods, bikers.

4. Petaluma KOA

Drive time from SF: 50 minutes · Hookups: Full · Cost: $80-110/night

The most family-friendly option on this list. KOA properties are reliable: pool, jumping pillow, mini-golf, dog park, on-site store, propane refill, laundry, and a small petting zoo with goats and chickens that kids love. The Petaluma KOA is on a working ranch, so views are rolling hills and pasture rather than dramatic nature, but the experience is incredibly relaxing.

Easy drive from anywhere in the Bay Area. Perfect base for visiting Sonoma wineries (though most are 30-45 minutes from the campground). Highway 101 access means you can hop up to Russian River or back to the city without much fuss.

Best for: Families with young kids (under 12), first-time RV families, anyone who wants amenities over wilderness.

5. Marin RV Park (San Rafael)

Drive time from SF: 30 minutes · Hookups: Full · Cost: $90-110/night

The closest full-hookup RV park to San Francisco that doesn't feel like a parking lot. Located in San Rafael, Marin RV Park has paved pads, clean restrooms, laundry, a pool in summer, and easy access to all of Marin's attractions: Mount Tamalpais, Stinson Beach, Muir Woods (10 miles), Sausalito (15 minutes).

This is a great choice if you want to use the RV as a home base while exploring the area in a separate car or on foot via the SMART train and ferry to the city.

Best for: Marin/SF day trips, anyone who wants comforts close to home, urban-adjacent camping.

6. Olema Campground

Drive time from SF: 1 hour 15 minutes · Hookups: Partial to full (varies by site) · Cost: $55-95/night

Right at the entrance to Point Reyes National Seashore. This is private land but the location can't be beat — you're on the doorstep of one of California's most stunning protected coastlines, with Tomales Bay, Drakes Beach, Limantour Beach, and the Point Reyes Lighthouse all within a 30-minute drive.

The campground itself is wooded and quiet. Wildlife is constant: tule elk on the peninsula, hawks overhead, sometimes whales offshore. Point Reyes Station is a charming 5-minute drive, with great restaurants (Cowgirl Creamery, Side Street Kitchen) and a Saturday farmers market.

Best for: Couples, photographers, anyone planning to explore Point Reyes for 2-3 days.

7. San Francisco RV Resort (Pacifica)

Drive time from SF: 20 minutes · Hookups: Full · Cost: $110-160/night

Right on the cliffs above Linda Mar Beach in Pacifica. This is the closest full-hookup RV resort to downtown San Francisco — you're 20 minutes from the city, with an unobstructed ocean view from every site on the bluff. Premium pricing reflects the location.

The setting is dramatic: surf below, Highway 1 winding south, sometimes fog rolling in over the cliffs. Pacifica itself has surf shops, the famous Pacifica Pier, and excellent taquerias. If you want a "luxury hotel" RV experience close to the city, this is it.

Best for: Couples, weekend trips, anyone wanting a city-adjacent escape without sacrificing views.

8. Bothe-Napa Valley State Park

Drive time from SF: 1 hour 30 minutes · Hookups: No · Cost: $35/night

Between Calistoga and St. Helena in the heart of Napa Valley. Sites are tucked among madrone and Douglas fir, with a creek and short hiking trails on-site. Despite being in the middle of one of California's most touristy regions, the campground feels remote and peaceful.

The obvious appeal is wine country access: dozens of wineries within 15 minutes, and Calistoga's hot springs and spa town vibes are 5 minutes away. Just remember that everyone needs to share driving duties — even modest wine tasting impairs driving an RV.

Best for: Wine country trips, couples, anyone wanting a budget-friendly alternative to Napa Valley hotels.

9. Henry Cowell Redwoods State Park

Drive time from SF: 1 hour 45 minutes · Hookups: No · Cost: $35/night

An old-growth redwood grove in the Santa Cruz Mountains, just outside Felton. The campground sits in second-growth redwoods, but a short walk gets you into a stand of 300-foot, 1,500-year-old trees. It's awe-inspiring without the crowds of Muir Woods.

Easy access to downtown Santa Cruz (20 minutes) for beach days and the boardwalk, or stay in the forest the whole time. Roaring Camp Railroad is on the doorstep — a steam train ride through the redwoods that kids love.

Best for: Families, redwood lovers, combining nature with a Santa Cruz beach day.

10. Big Basin Redwoods State Park

Drive time from SF: 2 hours · Hookups: No · Cost: $35/night

California's oldest state park, devastated by the 2020 CZU Lightning Complex fire but reopened to limited camping in 2024. The landscape is dramatically changed — many of the giant trees burned, though some old-growth survives — but the regrowth is a remarkable story of forest recovery. For RVers interested in seeing how a redwood ecosystem rebuilds itself, it's a meaningful trip.

Reserve well ahead and check the latest reopening status on Reserve California, as the park is rolling out access in phases. Hiking trails are being reopened gradually.

Best for: Curious travelers, photographers documenting recovery, anyone wanting a quieter alternative to the better-known redwood parks.

Tips for Any Bay Area RV Trip

A few notes that apply regardless of which campground you pick:

  • Book early for weekends, late for weekdays. Most coastal sites need 2-4 months advance booking for Saturday nights. Tuesday-Thursday sites often have same-week availability.
  • Check fire restrictions. California's fire season runs roughly May through November. Some campgrounds restrict campfires entirely during high-risk periods. Cooking on the RV stove is always fine.
  • Reserve California vs Recreation.gov: State parks use ReserveCalifornia.com. National parks (and some other federal sites) use Recreation.gov. Make sure you're booking on the right platform.
  • Tank stops matter. If you're staying at a no-hookup site for 3+ nights, plan to dump tanks somewhere before heading home. Most state parks have a free dump station near the entrance. Private RV parks often allow non-guests to dump for $10-20.
  • Cell service varies wildly. Verizon and AT&T cover most of these locations adequately. T-Mobile is spotty in West Marin and the redwoods. Pre-download maps before you leave town.

Which one should you pick?

If you've never RV'd before and want the lowest-stress trip possible: Pillar Point RV Park or Petaluma KOA. Full hookups, friendly staff, short drives.

If you want maximum nature for minimum effort: Samuel P. Taylor or Henry Cowell Redwoods. Old-growth redwoods, less than 2 hours from the city.

If you want an unforgettable view: Half Moon Bay State Beach or San Francisco RV Resort. Ocean cliffs and beach access.

If you have a long weekend: Olema Campground. Point Reyes deserves three days, not one.
